# Claimed! — P4 design summary

Claimed! helps MIT students buy and sell secondhand items through a shared marketplace. The final design connects discovery, exclusive reservation, negotiation, private messaging, and confirmation of payment made outside the app.

## From the P1 concept design

- **Keep separate responsibilities.** [P1's four concepts](https://github.com/SophiaCloutier/6.1040_Personal_Project/blob/a8368ff2bb92cc85f9191ca9c567829293da8805/Design/3.%20Concepts%20%26%20Reactions.md) became Listing, Claiming, Negotiating, and TransactionConfirming. Authenticating, Sessioning, ImageUploading, and Messaging handle supporting behavior independently. Reactions connect these concepts without putting the entire sale into one concept.
- **Make reservations authoritative.** P1 proposed accepting an offer before claiming the item. In the final design, selecting an offer reads its terms; the same atomic reservation used for a full-price purchase determines whether it succeeds. Only a successful reservation starts the transaction and closes competing offers. This prevents two buyers from securing one item. Pending offers do not reserve anything.
- **Preserve the agreement.** A reservation saves the agreed price, payment instructions, and proof requirement. Later listing edits cannot silently change those terms. Buyers report external payment; sellers confirm receipt. A screenshot can be optional or seller-required, but it does not replace seller confirmation or prove payment by itself.
- **Support changes of plan.** Either participant can cancel before payment is reported, releasing the item. Cancellation and payment reporting cannot both succeed. Sellers can edit listings with a check against stale details; payment reporting freezes edits. Sellers can remove available listings, while claimed and completed items retain their records. Completed sales leave browsing and remain in Buying and Selling history.
- **Simplify discovery and selling.** Tags replace fixed categories, with optional clothing sizes and combined filters. Up to five uploaded photos replace the original single image field. Sellers can frame cover photos, while buyers can inspect full images. The proposed free-with-purchase option was dropped because linking separate items would complicate reservations and cancellation.

The [final concept specifications](https://github.com/SophiaCloutier/6.1040_Personal_Project/tree/752e644ddd8eda04765029c7c6a6acaee5480f17/backend/design/concepts) and [reactions and HTTP compositions](https://github.com/SophiaCloutier/6.1040_Personal_Project/tree/752e644ddd8eda04765029c7c6a6acaee5480f17/backend/src/compositions) capture the implementation reviewed for P4.

## From the P3 visual design

- **Retain the established direction.** The final app keeps [P3's visual study](https://github.com/SophiaCloutier/6.1040_Personal_Project/blob/17e8cf3daeb79b8e191cf5b81e7ffcca482bee10/Design/6.%20Visual%20Study.md): cream and brown surfaces, rust actions, peach/pink/gold accents, wavy backgrounds, rounded controls, and arched listing photos. Cooper Black headings, Trebuchet MS controls, and Georgia item titles connect the interface to the retro thrifting references.
- **Separate shopping from transaction work.** A photo grid supports discovery, dedicated item pages hold details, and Buying, Selling, and Messages support ongoing transactions. Explicit status labels and payment steps communicate progress alongside color. Actionable sales appear before completed history.
- **Make targeted finishing changes.** After P3, public offer amounts let buyers see competing prices; an in-app back control improves navigation; available-listing removal gives sellers a way to withdraw an item; duplicate usernames receive a specific error. These address practical gaps without changing the visual system. No further visual redesign was needed for P4.

## Access control and validation

- Guests can browse; protected actions resolve identity from the session. Seller ownership controls edits, removal, and offer acceptance. The buyer reports payment and the seller confirms it. Payment instructions and evidence are restricted to the appropriate participants, and message history stays within its conversation. Public offer responses expose price information rather than private payment terms.
- Focused concept tests make principles and rejected actions visible separately; longer integration journeys remain to check reactions, persistence, competing claims, and HTTP access control together. The [P4 validation record](../validation/P4-validation.md) distinguishes these local checks from deployed-app evidence.
- Scope limits remain explicit: accounts do not verify MIT affiliation, payment happens externally, unused upload cleanup is deferred, and recovery from a crash between separate concept reactions is outside this version. These limits avoid implying guarantees the app does not provide.
