# Visual design study + annotations

## Overall direction

I want to go for a retro '70s vibe to match the thrifting element of Claimed! I really like the warm colors, wavy shapes, and chunky lettering of old posters and think it will make secondhand shopping feel fun and full of personality. I want to capture the feeling of finding something good at a thrift store, while keeping buying and selling easy and avoiding visual clutter.

## Slide 1: Color

![Color Slide](Slides\Color.png)

- **Wavy patterns and the orange/pink print:** I want to bring those flowing stripes into the background with peach, pink, and yellow ribbons. I feel like that will give the page some movement and that '70s feel.
- **Stamps and floral prints:** I want to pull in the vintage warmth of the earthy oranges and browns, with softer peach and pink surfaces for tags and smaller details.
- **“Pure Imagination” website:** I like how playful this feels while still having clear sections. I want to try that balance by putting the intro text on a translucent cream surface over the waves to keep it readable.
- **Palette:** I will use cream (`#fff3d9`) for the main background, dark brown (`#412b20`) for text, and a lighter brown (`#735b43`) for supporting details. Rust (`#a43e1d`) will draw attention to primary buttons and prices. Gold (`#f4b62d`) and peach (`#f8d5b3`) will round out the warm palette as accents.
- **Color with a job:** Filled rust buttons will make the main action easy to find. Availability labels and payment steps will also use words, so people won't have to figure out a transaction's status from color alone.



## Slide 2: Typography

![Typography Slide](Slides\Typography.png)

- **Cooper Black poster and “50 Retro Florals”:** I feel like the thick strokes and soft, rounded serifs will give the site that friendly retro character. I want to use Cooper Black for the logo and major headings. Also this is an iconc font of the 70's Hip-Hop movement and I think it will lend itself well to the retro-vintage vibe I am trying to curate.
- **Oldsmobile ad:** I want to borrow the contrast between the big serif headline and smaller supporting copy. Headings will help people get their bearings, while descriptions and transaction details will stay quieter.
- **Trebuchet MS:** I will use this as the main font for body text and controls. I feel like its open shapes and relatively large lowercase letters will keep practical information easy to read alongside the heavier headings.
- **Georgia and Times New Roman:** Georgia for listing titles and the italic phrase in the main headline to carry through the feel of the print references. Georgia and Times New Roman will also serve as fallbacks if Cooper Black isn't available.
